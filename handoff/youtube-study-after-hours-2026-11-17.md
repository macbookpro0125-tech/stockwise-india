**The lesson:** a famous consumer product, plus a yes/no question in the title, does best.

## How their best Short is built (Take-Two, 80 s)

1. **Hook (0–3 s):** the product, not the company ("GTA 6 comes out Nov 19", with the game's art).
2. **The move (3–10 s):** the company, plus one red/blue banner with the stock's move ("Up as much as 24% in under 2 weeks").
3. **The promise (10–20 s):** "We look at 3 things: ① Stage ② The right number ③ Its history". A numbered checklist card, so viewers know how long it is and stay.
4. **① Stage card:** three ticks or crosses:
   - Growing? (sales vs last year)
   - Making money? (profit or loss)
   - Paying shareholders back? (dividend or buyback)
5. **② The right number:** they choose the measure that fits the stage. A loss-maker gets price-to-sales, not P/E.
6. **③ Its history:** a 5-year line of that measure, with today's dot at the end and the usual level marked.
7. **A twist:** "Same report: the company's own forecast", then the number again on that forecast.
8. **Verdict card:** a one-line answer.
9. **Call to action:** "Run this on a stock you own: type your ticker in the comments." This brings comments, which bring reach, and it hands us the next topics.

**Look and feel:**
- White cards with a torn-paper edge, on a pale or dark grid with a faint moving line.
- Burned-in subtitles; the company's logo on each card.
- About 80 s with an even, calm voice.

**Description:** every figure with its date and source (SEC 8-K, 10-Q), the verdict in capitals, and "Not financial advice".

**Not for us:** verdicts like "cheap", "expensive", "bargain", "trap" or "price looks fair". Under our SEBI rule we say only where today's number sits against the company's own history ("above its usual", "near its 5-year low"), never what to do.

**What the winners have in common:**
1. **Something you buy:**
   - dog food, a momos plate, a Costco hot dog, a GTA game, Diwali crackers
   - "the real cost of X since 2010" and "how much profit in ₹40"
2. **A "how it really makes money" twist:** "the hot dog isn't the real product", "Zomato on every order".
3. **"What if you'd invested ₹1,000 in X"** in its common forms.
4. **One simple everyday comparison** that explains a measure (P/E as a lemonade stand).
5. **In India, Hinglish titles in Roman script do well:** "Paise kaise kamata hai?", "Kitna profit?"

**For us** (evergreen, from filings, no advice):
- "₹1,000 in Nestlé India 10 years ago" — a fact, not a tip; the past only.
- "How much of your ₹14 Maggi is Nestlé's profit?" — from its profit margin.
- "Asian Paints: the paint isn't the real product?"
- P/E explained with a chai stall.
- Hinglish titles alongside the English video, if the owner agrees (the voice stays English).
