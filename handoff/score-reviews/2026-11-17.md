## Rule fixed: one-off gains at loss-making companies (version 1.15)
The one-off-quarter rule (1.12) compared a quarter's profit with the company's usual quarter, using the *size* of past losses as "usual". So at a company with years of big losses, a large one-off gain looked normal and fed its P/E.
- **TTML** (Tata Teleservices Maharashtra): ₹581 Cr profit on ₹296 Cr of sales in the March 2026 quarter, after ₹150–325 Cr losses in every other quarter. Its P/E of 24.6 came from that one quarter, and its overall score was 58.8.
- **ANSALAPI**: ₹30 Cr profit on ₹17 Cr of sales (Dec 2025) after losses; P/E 1.1.

Change (server/metrics.js `ttmOneOff`): a quarter whose profit is above its own sales also counts as a one-off when it is over 4× the usual quarter's profit, with losses counted as no profit. Holding companies whose profit always tops their sales (Bajaj Holdings) are not caught, because their usual quarter is itself a large profit. A first, wider version (losses as no profit in the old test too) also caught investment companies whose profits swing both ways (Dhunseri Investments, VLS Finance) and KCP Sugar, so it was dropped.

Effect: 24 companies get a one-off quarter, almost all tiny or not scored. **3 overall scores moved:** TTML 58.8 → quality view only (no profit without the one-off), ONELIFECAP 30.3 → quality view only (₹12.7 Cr profit on ₹3.9 Cr of sales, mostly other income, between losses), SHAHALLOYS 16.7 → 13 (a second one-off quarter). P/E-under-2 flags 7 → 5 (ANSALAPI and SVPGLOB cleared). Tests in server/research-test.js: a loss-maker's gain is caught (fails on the old rule); a holding company is not. RESEARCH_VERSION 1.15.

Past-price test re-run: the top fifth was still ahead of the bottom fifth on 6 of 7 dates after 12 months. The typical company's change is now −0.8% against −8.1% (was −1.0% against −7.8%), so the methodology page was updated.
