## Live (9 Nov, night)
- Pushed 9 Nov at `2a1a2cc` (158 passed; live 22:00 IST, 83s after the push): the phone tab bar's bubble made smooth (the owner: "not that smooth, it looks laggy"). Two causes, measured in a phone-sized Chrome slowed 4×: each frame of the drop took ~18ms of the 16.7 it has (the rim lens worked out pixel by pixel in JavaScript, every tab's cut-out redrawn, the page asked where the tabs were each frame), and a tap built the new page in one go just as the drop moved fastest (held still 50–100ms). Now the rim lens is drawn by the graphics chip (WebGL in src/rimLens.js; the pixel loop stays as the fallback and gives the same pixels), the tabs' places are measured once per layout, only the tabs under the drop are cut, a style is written only when it changes (src/liquidLens.js), and a page opened from the tab bar is built in the background a slice at a time (App.jsx goTab's `glide`: startTransition; it shows at its top once ready; a tab's page code is fetched when a finger touches it). Every other way of changing page (a computer's bar, links, Back) switches at once, as before. Slowed 4×: the drop alone draws 36 of 36 frames (was 33), its own work 0.4ms a frame (was ~15); a tab switch 33 of 36 (was 29), longest pause 43ms (was 75). The new page shows as soon as before (34ms after the finger lifts at full speed). The motion is unchanged (path, sizes, timing). What's left is Discover's own start-up (the night-sky header restyles the whole page; its charts re-measure), after the drop has landed at full speed. Test N-H3 also accepts the app's "too large to read" answer (a 15.65 MB filing was the newest result on 9 Nov and failed it on the pushed code too). Not yet checked on a real iPhone; Safari keeps page animation at 60fps even on 120Hz iPhones unless its "Prefer Page Rendering Updates near 60fps" setting is off.
